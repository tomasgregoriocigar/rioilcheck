== COD vs FULL-SERVICE: WHAT THE GAP BUYS ==

The same No. 2 heating oil, delivered out of the same marine terminals, sells at two very different prices in Rhode Island — because the per-gallon price is only part of what a full-service dealer sells. Neither way of buying is “the smart one” for everyone. The honest question is whether you use what the gap pays for.

FULL-SERVICE (AROUND THE STATE AVERAGE)

What the higher per-gallon price buys:

  • 24/7 emergency service and a service contract — a no-heat call at 2 AM is covered, not a scramble.
  • Automatic delivery — the dealer tracks your usage and fills the tank before it runs dry (this is the “keep-full” basis the state survey prices).
  • Budget / equal-payment plans — winter’s cost spread across the year instead of landing in a few large fills.
  • Credit terms — delivery now, bill on account (note: 30-day billing typically runs 5–10¢/gal above prompt-pay pricing, per Green Energy Consumers Alliance).
  • Price-protection options — cap and pre-buy plans are usually full-service products.

COD / WILL-CALL (WELL UNDER THE AVERAGE)

What you give up — and take on yourself:

  • You watch the tank. Nobody fills it automatically; run it dry and you may pay for an emergency delivery and a burner prime/restart.
  • You pay up front — cash on delivery or prepay, per fill, at the day’s price.
  • No service contract. Repairs and annual tune-ups are arranged and paid for separately.
  • Minimum deliveries apply — discount pricing is typically quoted for a set fill size (the October 2026 postings above were priced on 150-gallon orders).
  • The fuel is identical. The gap does not buy better oil — it buys the service bundle in the other column.

> WHO SHOULD BUY WHICH WAY

  • Full-service fits if your equipment is older or unreliable, you cannot easily monitor the tank (travel, tenants, a second home), or you need winter costs smoothed into a budget plan and value one phone number when the heat stops.
  • COD fits if your equipment is newer and serviced, you are comfortable watching the gauge and timing fills, and you can pay per fill. The documented gap — roughly 30–70¢/gal at October 2026 levels — is real money across a winter.
  • The middle path: a buying group such as Green Energy Consumers Alliance pairs members with a full-service dealer at a capped margin — documented savings of 10–50¢/gal vs the state survey average, with the service bundle kept.

WATCH OUT: Comparing a COD posting to your full-service bill — or the reverse — without naming the basis is how households get misled. Always ask which of these a quoted price is, what the delivery minimum is, and what fees sit on top of the per-gallon price, before you order.

== PROPANE: BENCHMARK + YOUR RIGHTS UNDER RI LAW ==

> YOUR RIGHTS UNDER RI LAW — RI GEN. LAWS §5-83

Propane is the one delivered fuel where Rhode Island law gives you explicit disclosure rights. Most propane customers do not know they have them:

  • Fees above the delivered per-gallon cost are not allowed unless disclosed in advance and itemized on the invoice. A propane dealer may not charge fees above the delivered cost per gallon unless they were disclosed to you in advance and appear, itemized, on your invoice (RI Gen. Laws §5-83-2).
  • On request, your dealer must disclose the price. Ask, and the dealer must tell you the estimated price per gallon and the applicable charges (§5-83-2).
  • Guaranteed-price and prepaid contracts have legal terms too — the same chapter covers them (§5-83-1), and violations of the chapter carry a fine of up to $500 (§5-83-6).

USE IT: Before any propane delivery, ask for the estimated price per gallon and every applicable charge — it is your right under §5-83-2, not a favor. After delivery, check that any fee above the per-gallon cost was disclosed in advance and is itemized on the invoice. If a dealer will not do either, you can file a complaint with the RI Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Unit: riag.ri.gov/forms/consumer-complaint.

NO EQUIVALENT FOR HEATING OIL: There is no disclosure statute like §5-83 for heating oil. For oil, your protection is the benchmark on this page, a direct question to the dealer before you order, and the same AG complaint route if a dealer misleads you.

== HELP PAYING FOR HEAT ==

If a fill is out of reach, there is help that does not cost you anything and does not sell you anything. This site makes no money from this section and never will.

  • LIHEAP — Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program. Federal grants that help eligible Rhode Island households pay heating costs, administered by the RI Department of Human Services (DHS) through local Community Action Agencies. There are two parts: heating assistance (a grant toward your heating costs, usually paid to your fuel vendor) and crisis assistance (help with a shut-off, an inability to pay for a fuel delivery, or a heating system failure that cannot be repaired). Households at or below 60% of Rhode Island’s median income are income-eligible; you do not need to be on public assistance or behind on a bill to qualify, and both renters and homeowners can apply. The FFY 2027 application period opened October 1, 2026 — apply early, through DHS online or your local Community Action Agency: RI DHS LIHEAP.
  • Do not let the tank run dry while you wait. If you are facing a no-heat crisis, say so when you apply — crisis assistance exists specifically for deliverable-fuel emergencies and unrepairable heating-system failures.
  • Something wrong with a bill or a delivery? The RI Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Unit accepts complaints about businesses operating in Rhode Island: file a consumer complaint.

== FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS ==

What is the state average price?

The state average is the Rhode Island residential heating oil price published by the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) under the State Heating Oil and Propane Program (SHOPP), a joint survey run with state energy offices on Form EIA-877. For the week of October 12, 2026 it was $6.304 per gallon. It is a statewide survey average of the residential credit price charged by full-service dealers, excluding taxes — one average for the whole state. SHOPP does not publish county-level or dealer-level prices, and the average may be revised in the following week’s release.

Is the state average the price I should pay?

No. The state average is a benchmark — the price to measure a quote against — not a quote and not a target. It tracks the full-service, on-credit price, which is the top of the market’s normal range. If you buy from a full-service dealer and use its service contract, automatic delivery, and credit terms, paying around the average can be fair. If you buy COD (cash on delivery, will-call), a fair price should be well under the average, because you are not buying the service bundle. Green Energy Consumers Alliance documents that its buying-group members typically save 10–50 cents per gallon compared to the state survey average.

Why is my dealer’s price higher than a COD price?

Because the two prices buy different things. A full-service price bundles the fuel with 24/7 emergency service, a service contract, automatic delivery so you do not have to watch the tank gauge, budget or equal-payment plans, and credit terms. A COD price is fuel only: you watch your own tank, call for each delivery yourself, and pay up front. The fuel itself is the same No. 2 heating oil. The gap between the two is the price of the service bundle — worth paying only if you actually use the services. See COD vs FULL-SERVICE above.

Do propane dealers have to tell me their price per gallon?

Yes — on request. Under Rhode Island law (RI Gen. Laws §5-83-2), a propane dealer must, on request, disclose the estimated price per gallon and applicable charges. A propane dealer also may not charge fees above the delivered cost per gallon unless those fees were disclosed in advance and are itemized on the invoice. There is no equivalent disclosure statute for heating oil.

How often does the benchmark update?

During the heating season, October through March, SHOPP prices are collected as of each Monday and published by EIA on Wednesdays at 1:00 PM Eastern. From April through September, prices are collected only on the second Monday of each month, and no monthly averages are published for that period. The prior week’s average may be revised in the next release to account for late submissions and corrections — so treat each week’s number as the best official figure, not a permanent one.

Does Rhode Island regulate heating oil prices?

No. The RI Office of Energy Resources states: “Rhode Island does not regulate retail sales of heating oil and propane. Sales are managed primarily by local fuel dealers.” That is exactly why the same fuel can sell at materially different prices on the same day — and why a public benchmark matters. (Over one-third of Rhode Island homes heat with oil and propane, per the same OER page.) Propane customers do have specific disclosure rights under RI Gen. Laws §5-83, and any consumer can file a complaint with the RI Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Unit.
